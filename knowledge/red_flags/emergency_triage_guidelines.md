# Emergency Clinical Red Flags

## Immediate Emergency Indicators Requiring Emergent Medical Evaluation

### Cardiovascular & Hemodynamic Emergencies
- Severe crushing chest pain radiating to arm, jaw, or back, associated with diaphoresis or shortness of breath.
- Sustained hypotension (Systolic BP < 90 mmHg) with signs of hypoperfusion (pallor, altered mental status, oliguria).
- Malignant hypertension (Systolic BP > 180 mmHg or Diastolic BP > 120 mmHg) with end-organ dysfunction.

### Neurological Emergencies
- Sudden focal neurological deficits: acute hemiparesis, facial asymmetry, sudden severe aphasia, or ataxia (FAST criteria).
- "Thunderclap" headache reaching maximal intensity within seconds (suspected subarachnoid hemorrhage).
- Sudden unexplained loss of consciousness (syncope) or status epilepticus.

### Respiratory Emergencies
- Severe acute respiratory distress: respiratory rate > 30/min, inability to complete full sentences, severe stridor.
- Severe hypoxemia: Pulse oximetry SpO2 < 90% on room air.
- Massive hemoptysis (>100 mL blood expectorated in 24 hours).

### Acute Abdominal Emergencies
- Acute rigid, board-like abdomen with severe rebound tenderness and involuntary guarding (peritonitis/perforation).
- Severe progressive right lower quadrant pain with fever and rebound tenderness (acute appendicitis).

## System Triage Rule
Whenever any of these red flags are detected in clinical narratives or structured inputs, the system must immediately trigger a prominent critical warning and prioritize urgent in-person emergency evaluation over routine recommendations.
