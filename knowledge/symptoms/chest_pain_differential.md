# Chest Pain Clinical Differential Diagnosis

## Overview & Risk Stratification
Chest pain is one of the most common acute clinical presentations, requiring rapid distinction between life-threatening emergent etiologies and benign non-cardiac conditions.

## The 6 Life-Threatening Causes of Acute Chest Pain ("The Big Six")
1. **Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI / Unstable Angina)**: Retrosternal pressure, diaphoresis, radiation to arm/jaw, exertional.
2. **Aortic Dissection**: Sudden severe tearing/ripping chest/back pain, blood pressure differential between arms, pulse deficits.
3. **Pulmonary Embolism**: Acute dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, hypoxia, DVT signs.
4. **Tension Pneumothorax**: Unilateral decreased breath sounds, hyperresonance, tracheal deviation, hemodynamic collapse.
5. **Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave Syndrome)**: Severe retrosternal pain post-vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema, mediastinal crunch (Hamman sign).
6. **Cardiac Tamponade**: Beck triad (hypotension, jugular venous distention, muffled heart sounds), pulsus paradoxus.

## Common Non-Emergent Etiologies
- **Gastrointestinal**: GERD, esophageal spasm, peptic ulcer disease, biliary colic.
- **Musculoskeletal**: Costochondritis (reproducible localized tenderness on chest wall palpation).
- **Psychiatric**: Panic disorder / acute hyperventilation syndrome.
- **Pulmonary**: Acute bronchitis, pleurisy, pneumonia.
