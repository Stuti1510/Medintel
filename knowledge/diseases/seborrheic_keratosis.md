# Seborrheic Keratosis (SEK)

## Definition
Seborrheic keratosis (SEK) is one of the most common completely benign non-cancerous skin tumors in older adults. It originates from clonal proliferation of epidermal keratinocytes and exhibits no malignant potential.

## Clinical Appearance
- Well-demarcated, "stuck-on" appearance, resembling a drop of melted candle wax or dry oatmeal stuck to the cutaneous surface.
- Verrucous (warty), velvety, smooth, or hyperkeratotic crumbly surface texture.
- Color ranges from pale yellow, light tan, rich brown, to deep uniform black.
- Multiple crypts, comedo-like openings, and milia-like cysts often clearly identifiable on dermoscopic examination.
- Ranges from a few millimeters to several centimeters in diameter.

## Typical Symptoms & Presentation
- Generally asymptomatic, slow-growing, and benign throughout its natural course.
- Can become inflamed, itchy, irritated, or bleed when subjected to mechanical friction from clothing, bra straps, belts, or scratching.
- Most commonly found on the trunk (back and chest), neck, face, scalp, and extremities (strictly sparing palms and soles).
- Typically manifests in individuals over the age of 40-50, increasing in frequency and number with advancing age.

## Red Flags
- A rapidly growing, deeply pigmented, solitary or atypical lesion that can visually or dermoscopically mimic pigmented melanoma or BCC.
- Sudden explosive eruptive onset of hundreds of pruritic seborrheic keratoses over weeks to months (Sign of Leser-Trélat), which may serve as a cutaneous paraneoplastic marker of underlying internal gastrointestinal or pulmonary adenocarcinoma.
- Spontaneous bleeding, deep ulceration, or marked morphological asymmetry requiring formal clinical and dermoscopic differentiation from malignant lesions.

## When to See a Dermatologist
- Any newly arising or changing dark warty bump to rule out cutaneous malignancy via clinical dermoscopy.
- Lesions causing chronic mechanical irritation, recurrent trauma, bleeding, or cosmetic distress can be safely removed by cryotherapy, curettage, or shave excision.
