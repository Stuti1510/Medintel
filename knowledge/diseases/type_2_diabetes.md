# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)

## Overview & Pathophysiology
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus is a progressive metabolic disorder characterized by peripheral insulin resistance, inadequate compensatory insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells, and hepatic glucose overproduction.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Classic Triad**: Polyuria (frequent urination), Polydipsia (excessive thirst), Polyphagia (excessive hunger).
- **Secondary Manifestations**: Unintentional weight loss, blurred vision, chronic fatigue, slow-healing wounds, recurrent skin/candida infections, and peripheral neuropathy (glove-and-stocking paresthesias).

## Diagnostic Criteria (ADA)
1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)**: ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after an 8-hour fast.
2. **Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)**: ≥6.5% using a standardized NGSP assay.
3. **2-Hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)**: ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L).
4. **Random Plasma Glucose**: ≥200 mg/dL in a patient with classic hyperglycemic crisis symptoms.

## Clinical Guidance & Longitudinal Management
- First-line lifestyle modification: medical nutrition therapy, structured aerobic/resistance physical activity, weight loss goals.
- Pharmacotherapy: Metformin as cornerstone therapy alongside GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors for patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease.
- Comprehensive microvascular screening: annual dilated eye exam, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR), and comprehensive diabetic foot exam.
