## Overview & Etiology
Hyperthyroidism refers to excessive synthesis and secretion of thyroid hormones (T3, T4) by the thyroid gland. The most common etiology is Graves' disease (autoimmune stimulation of TSH receptors), followed by toxic multinodular goiter and toxic adenoma.

## Cardinal Signs, Symptoms & Labs
- Hypermetabolic symptoms: Heat intolerance, palpitations, weight loss despite increased appetite, fine tremor of outstretched hands, emotional lability, proximal muscle weakness, frequent bowel movements.
- Physical signs: Resting tachycardia, systolic hypertension, warm moist skin, hyperreflexia, exophthalmos / proptosis (Graves' orbitopathy), diffuse thyroid enlargement with audible bruit.
- Laboratory confirmation: Suppressed serum TSH (<0.01 mIU/L), elevated Free T4 and/or Free T3. TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb) confirm Graves' disease.

## Treatment Protocols
- Symptomatic relief: Beta-blockers (propranolol, atenolol) for adrenergic symptoms (tachycardia, tremor).
- Antithyroid medications: Methimazole (preferred initial agent) or Propylthiouracil (PTU, preferred in first trimester of pregnancy).
- Definitive therapy: Radioactive iodine ablation (131-I) or total thyroidectomy.
