## Overview & Pathophysiology
Atopic dermatitis (eczema) is a chronic, relapsing, pruritic inflammatory skin disease associated with skin barrier dysfunction (filaggrin gene mutations) and immune dysregulation (predominantly Th2-skewed cytokine responses).

## Diagnostic Criteria & Clinical Manifestations
- Essential features: Pruritus (itching) and eczematous lesions (acute erythema, excoriation, or chronic lichenification).
- Age-specific predilection: Flexural involvement in children and adults (antecubital and popliteal fossae, neck, wrists).
- Associated atopic diathesis: Personal or family history of asthma, allergic rhinitis, or food allergies.
- Laboratory evaluation: Elevated total serum IgE, peripheral eosinophilia during exacerbations.

## Stepwise Therapeutic Strategy
- Baseline barrier restoration: Emollients, gentle skin cleansers, trigger avoidance.
- Acute flare control: Topical corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, clobetasol) or topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus).
- Moderate-to-severe disease: Targeted biologic therapy (dupilumab, tralokinumab) or oral JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib, abrocitinib).
