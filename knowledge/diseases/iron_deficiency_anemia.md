## Overview & Pathogenesis
Iron deficiency anemia (IDA) develops when total body iron stores become depleted, compromising hemoglobin synthesis and oxygen transport capacity. In adult men and postmenopausal women, occult gastrointestinal bleeding is the primary cause until proven otherwise.

## Hematological & Biochemical Profiles
- Cardinal symptoms: Progressive fatigue, exertional dyspnea, pallor (conjunctival, palmar), brittle nails with koilonychia, angular cheilitis, pica (craving ice/clay).
- Complete blood count: Low hemoglobin (<13.0 g/dL in men, <12.0 g/dL in non-pregnant women), microcytosis (low MCV < 80 fL), hypochromia (low MCH < 27 pg), elevated RDW.
- Iron studies: Serum ferritin < 30 mcg/L (highly specific), low transferrin saturation (TSAT < 20%), elevated total iron-binding capacity (TIBC).

## Clinical Management
- Oral iron therapy: Ferrous sulfate (324 mg daily or alternate-day dosing) with ascorbic acid to enhance absorption.
- Intravenous iron: Indicated for oral intolerance, severe anemia (Hb < 7-8 g/dL), malabsorption (celiac disease, post-bariatric surgery), or active inflammatory bowel disease.
- Endoscopic evaluation: Upper endoscopy and colonoscopy recommended to evaluate GI blood loss in appropriate cohorts.
