## Overview & Pathophysiology
Infectious mononucleosis is most commonly caused by Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), a human gammaherpesvirus that infects B lymphocytes via CD21 receptors, eliciting a robust polyclonal CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocyte response.

## Clinical Triad & Diagnostic Markers
- Classical triad: Exudative pharyngitis / sore throat, fever (often prolonged 1-2 weeks), and symmetric posterior cervical lymphadenopathy.
- Associated signs: Splenomegaly (in 50% of cases), palatal petechiae, periorbital edema, profound fatigue lasting weeks.
- Laboratory confirmation: Atypical lymphocytosis on peripheral blood smear (>=10%), positive heterophile antibody test (Monospot), or positive EBV viral capsid antigen (VCA) IgM antibodies.

## Management & Complications
- Primary care: Supportive therapy with hydration, NSAIDs or acetaminophen for fever and pain, adequate rest.
- Activity restriction: Strict avoidance of contact sports and heavy lifting for at least 3 to 4 weeks to prevent splenic rupture.
- Corticosteroid use: Reserved exclusively for impending airway compromise, severe autoimmune hemolytic anemia, or thrombocytopenia.
