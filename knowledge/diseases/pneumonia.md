# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

## Overview & Pathophysiology
Pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma commonly caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, atypical pathogens (*Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*), respiratory viruses, or gram-negative bacilli.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Respiratory Symptoms**: Productive cough with purulent or rust-colored sputum, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain on inspiration.
- **Systemic Signs**: High spiking fever (>38.5°C), rigors, diaphoresis, malaise, and tachycardia.
- **Physical Examination**: Dullness to chest percussion, bronchial breath sounds, crackles (rales), and increased tactile fremitus over consolidated zones.

## Diagnostic Workup & Risk Stratification
1. **Chest Radiography / CT**: Demonstrates lobar consolidation, patchy alveolar infiltrates, or air bronchograms.
2. **CURB-65 Score**: Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30/min, Blood pressure (SBP <90 or DBP ≤60 mmHg), Age ≥65 years.
3. **Microbiology**: Sputum Gram stain and culture, blood cultures (in severe/hospitalized cases), urinary antigen tests for *Legionella* and *Pneumococcus*.

## Clinical Guidance
- Outpatient vs. inpatient disposition guided by clinical judgment and CURB-65/PSI scores.
- Empiric antimicrobial therapy directed against common bacterial and atypical pathogens.
- Adequate hydration, pulmonary toilet, and monitor for respiratory failure or pleural effusion/empyema.
