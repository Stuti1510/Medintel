## Overview & Immunopathogenesis
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease characterized by symmetric inflammatory polyarthritis, synovial hyperplasia (pannus formation), and progressive cartilage and bone destruction mediated by TNF-alpha, IL-6, and autoantibodies.

## Clinical Manifestations & Classification (ACR/EULAR 2010)
- Joint involvement: Symmetric small joint polyarthritis (MCP, PIP, wrists, MTP joints), sparing the DIP joints.
- Cardinal signs: Morning stiffness lasting > 1 hour, joint swelling, tenderness, warmth, rheumatoid nodules.
- Serological markers: Rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies.
- Acute phase reactants: Elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and C-reactive protein (CRP).

## Treat-to-Target Therapeutic Strategy
- Initial DMARD therapy: Methotrexate (oral or subcutaneous, 15-25 mg weekly) combined with folic acid supplementation.
- Glucocorticoid bridge: Low-dose oral prednisone (<10 mg daily) for acute symptom relief during DMARD initiation.
- Biologic / Targeted synthetic DMARDs: TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, etanercept), IL-6 receptor antagonists (tocilizumab), or JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib) for inadequate response to conventional synthetic DMARDs.
