# Pulmonary Embolism (PE)

## Overview & Pathophysiology
Pulmonary embolism occurs when deep venous thrombi (typically from lower extremity deep veins) detach and embolize into the pulmonary arterial bed, causing vascular occlusion, acute right ventricular strain, and ventilation-perfusion mismatch.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Cardinal Symptoms**: Acute onset dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachypnea (>20 breaths/min), tachycardia (>100 bpm), hemoptysis, and syncope.
- **Signs of DVT**: Unilateral lower extremity swelling, erythema, tenderness, and warmth.
- **Hemodynamic Instability**: Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg) or cardiogenic shock signaling massive high-risk PE.

## Diagnostic Algorithm
1. **Clinical Probability Assessment**: Wells Score or Revised Geneva Score.
2. **D-Dimer Assay**: High sensitivity ELISA to rule out PE in low/intermediate probability patients.
3. **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**: Definitive gold-standard diagnostic imaging modality.
4. **Bedside Echocardiography**: Assesses RV dilation, hypokinesis (McConnell sign), and pulmonary hypertension.

## Clinical Guidance & Interventions
- Immediate parenteral anticoagulation (LMWH, fondaparinux, or unfractionated heparin) while awaiting diagnostic confirmation in high-suspicion cases.
- Primary reperfusion therapy (systemic thrombolysis or catheter-directed thrombectomy) for hemodynamically unstable massive PE.
- Transition to direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs: apixaban, rivaroxaban) for maintenance therapy.
