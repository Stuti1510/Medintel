# Bronchial Asthma

## Overview & Pathophysiology
Asthma is a heterogeneous chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by variable expiratory airflow limitation and respiratory symptoms resulting from airway hyperresponsiveness and remodeling.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Hallmark Symptoms**: Recurrent episodes of wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough (predominantly nocturnal or early morning).
- **Triggers**: Viral infections, allergens (dust mites, pollen, animal dander), cold air, exercise, chemical irritants, and NSAIDs/beta-blockers.
- **Physical Signs**: Expiratory wheezing on chest auscultation, prolonged expiratory phase, and tachypnea during exacerbations.

## Diagnostic Confirmation
1. **Spirometry**: Demonstrates airflow limitation ($FEV_1/FVC < 0.70$) with significant bronchodilator reversibility ($\Delta FEV_1 > 12\%$ and $>200$ mL post-SABA).
2. **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF)**: Diurnal variability $>10\%$.
3. **Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO)**: Evaluates eosinophilic/Type 2 airway inflammation.

## Clinical Guidance
- Stepwise management following GINA pathways emphasizing inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)-formoterol as preferred reliever and controller.
- Elimination of environmental triggers and personalized written asthma action plans.
- Severe refractory asthma evaluation for biological therapies (anti-IgE, anti-IL5, anti-IL4R).
