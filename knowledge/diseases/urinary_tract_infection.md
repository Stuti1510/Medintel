## Overview & Microbiology
Urinary tract infections (UTIs) encompass acute uncomplicated cystitis and acute pyelonephritis. Over 75-90% of uncomplicated cases are caused by uropathogenic Escherichia coli (UPEC), followed by Klebsiella pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis, and Staphylococcus saprophyticus.

## Diagnostic Signs & Laboratory Evaluation
- Acute cystitis symptoms: Dysuria, urinary frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain, cloudy/malodorous urine, hematuria.
- Acute pyelonephritis features: Flank pain, costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness, fever (>38°C), chills, nausea, vomiting.
- Urinalysis (dipstick/microscopy): Positive leukocyte esterase, positive nitrites (gram-negative enterics), pyuria (>=10 WBC/hpf), bacteriuria.
- Urine culture: Confirmatory with >= 10^5 CFU/mL (or >= 10^3 CFU/mL in symptomatic females).

## Empirical Antimicrobial Stewardship
- First-line acute cystitis: Nitrofurantoin (100 mg BID for 5 days), Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX, 160/800 mg BID for 3 days if local resistance <20%), or Fosfomycin trometamol (3 g single dose).
- Pyelonephritis therapy: Oral ciprofloxacin / levofloxacin or IV ceftriaxone based on severity and local susceptibility patterns.
