## Overview & Clinical Presentation
Acute bronchitis is a self-limiting inflammation of the large airways (bronchi) characterized by cough with or without sputum production, lasting up to 3 to 4 weeks. Over 90% of cases are caused by viral pathogens (influenza A and B, parainfluenza, coronavirus, rhinovirus, RSV).

## Diagnostic Evaluation & Cardinal Signs
- Cardinal symptom: Persistent cough lasting > 5 days (often 1-3 weeks), purulent or clear sputum.
- Associated symptoms: Low-grade fever, malaise, chest wall tenderness, wheezing or coarse rhonchi.
- Distinction from Pneumonia: Absence of tachypnea (>24/min), tachycardia (>100 bpm), high fever (>38°C), or focal consolidation signs (egophony, tactile fremitus, dullness to percussion).
- Diagnostic testing: Chest X-ray is reserved for patients with abnormal vital signs or focal lung findings to rule out pneumonia.

## Management & Treatment Guidelines
- First-line: Supportive care (hydration, rest, antitussives/dextromethorphan, bronchodilators for patients with underlying airway hyperreactivity).
- Antibiotic stewardship: Routine antibiotic therapy is not recommended for uncomplicated acute bronchitis in immunocompetent individuals.
