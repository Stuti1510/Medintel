# Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

## Overview & Pathophysiology
GERD develops when reflux of gastric contents causes troublesome symptoms and/or mucosal complications, primarily driven by transient lower esophageal sphincter relaxations (TLESRs), hypotensive LES, or hiatal hernia.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Esophageal Symptoms**: Retrosternal burning sensation (heartburn/pyrosis) exacerbated postprandially and by recumbency; acid regurgitation; water brash.
- **Extraesophageal Manifestations**: Chronic nocturnal cough, reflux laryngitis/hoarseness, asthma-like wheezing, globus sensation, dental enamel erosion.
- **Alarm Features**: Progressive dysphagia, odynophagia, unintentional weight loss, hematemesis, or melena (requires emergent upper endoscopy).

## Diagnostic Workup
1. **Empiric Trial**: 8-week course of once-daily Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) for typical symptoms.
2. **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)**: Evaluates for reflux esophagitis (Los Angeles classification), Barrett esophagus, and strictures.
3. **Ambulatory Reflux Monitoring**: 24-hour pH-impedance testing to confirm acid exposure time (AET) in refractory cases.

## Clinical Guidance
- Lifestyle interventions: Weight loss in overweight/obese patients, elevation of head of bed, avoidance of late meals (within 2–3 hours of sleeping), and trigger avoidance (fatty meals, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol).
- Pharmacotherapy: Standard-dose PPIs taken 30–60 minutes before first meal of the day; H2-receptor antagonists for nocturnal breakthrough.
