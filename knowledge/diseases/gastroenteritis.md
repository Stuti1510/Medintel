## Overview & Pathophysiology
Acute gastroenteritis is an infectious syndrome characterized by the sudden onset of diarrhea (>= 3 loose stools per day) with or without vomiting, fever, abdominal cramps, and tenesmus, typically lasting less than 14 days. Common etiologies include norovirus, rotavirus, Campylobacter, Salmonella, and enterotoxigenic E. coli.

## Clinical Assessment & Dehydration Severity
- Symptoms: Watery diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, diffuse crampy abdominal pain, low-grade pyrexia.
- Dehydration grading: Mild (increased thirst, dry mucous membranes), Moderate (sunken eyes, decreased skin turgor, tachycardia), Severe (hypotension, lethargy, oliguria).
- Red flags: Bloody stools (dysentery), severe localized abdominal tenderness, high spiking fevers (>38.5°C), or immunocompromised state.

## Therapeutic Protocols
- Primary therapy: Oral rehydration solution (ORS) with balanced electrolytes and glucose; IV crystalloid fluids (lactated Ringer's or normal saline) for severe dehydration.
- Symptom relief: Antiemetics (ondansetron) for refractory vomiting. Antimotility agents (loperamide) are contraindicated in inflammatory/bloody diarrhea.
- Antimicrobial therapy: Reserved for confirmed bacterial pathogens (azithromycin or fluoroquinolones for severe dysenteric illness).
