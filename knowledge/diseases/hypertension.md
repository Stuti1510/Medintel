## Overview & Classification
Hypertension is defined as persistent elevation of systemic arterial pressure (systolic BP >= 130 mmHg or diastolic BP >= 80 mmHg on repeated standardized clinical measurements). It is the leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease.

## Diagnostic Categories & Target Organ Damage
- Stage 1 Hypertension: SBP 130-139 mmHg or DBP 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 Hypertension: SBP >= 140 mmHg or DBP >= 90 mmHg.
- Hypertensive Crisis: SBP > 180 mmHg and/or DBP > 120 mmHg (Urgency without organ damage vs. Emergency with acute encephalopathy, aortic dissection, or acute pulmonary edema).
- Clinical signs: Morning occipital headache, dizziness, exertional dyspnea, blurred vision, epistaxis.
- Target organ assessment: Funduscopic exam (retinopathy), serum creatinine / eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR), 12-lead ECG (left ventricular hypertrophy).

## Pharmacological & Lifestyle Management
- First-line antihypertensive classes: Thiazide diuretics (chlorthalidone, hydrochlorothiazide), ACE inhibitors (lisinopril), ARBs (losartan), and DHP calcium channel blockers (amlodipine).
- Combination therapy: Recommended as initial therapy in Stage 2 hypertension.
- Target blood pressure: < 130/80 mmHg in most adults with confirmed hypertension.
