# Acute Appendicitis

## Overview & Pathophysiology
Acute appendicitis is an acute inflammation of the vermiform appendix, initiated by luminal obstruction from fecaliths, lymphoid hyperplasia, or foreign bodies, leading to increased intraluminal pressure, ischemia, and bacterial invasion.

## Key Clinical Findings
- **Pain Migration**: Periumbilical visceral pain migrating and localizing to the Right Lower Quadrant (RLQ) over 12–24 hours.
- **Physical Signs**: McBurney point tenderness, Rovsing sign (RLQ pain on LLQ palpation), Psoas and Obturator signs, involuntary guarding, and rebound tenderness.
- **Systemic Symptoms**: Anorexia ("hamburger sign"), low-grade fever, nausea, and mild vomiting following pain onset.

## Diagnostic Workup & Alvarado Score
1. **Clinical Scoring**: Alvarado Score (MANTRELS) or Appendicitis Inflammatory Response (AIR) score.
2. **Ultrasound / Abdominal CT**: Graded compression ultrasound (first-line in children/pregnant patients); contrast-enhanced CT showing non-compressible dilated appendix (>6 mm diameter) with wall thickening and periappendiceal fat stranding.
3. **Laboratory**: Leukocytosis with left shift, elevated C-reactive protein.

## Clinical Guidance
- Prompt surgical consultation for laparoscopic appendectomy.
- Preoperative IV broad-spectrum antibiotics and fluid resuscitation.
- Vigilant monitoring for perforation, peritonitis, or periappendiceal abscess.
