# Actinic Keratosis (ACK)

## Definition
Actinic keratosis (ACK, also called solar keratosis) is a common pre-malignant cutaneous intraepithelial lesion caused by chronic ultraviolet (UV) radiation-induced DNA damage to epidermal keratinocytes. It represents part of a biological continuum that can progress to invasive squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) if left untreated.

## Clinical Appearance
- Rough, dry, gritty, sand-paper-like scaly erythematous papules or macules ranging from 2 mm to over 1 cm in diameter.
- Color varies from flesh-colored, yellow-brown, pink-red, to hyperkeratotic brown.
- Often easier to identify by gentle palpation ("feels like fine sandpaper") than by visual inspection alone.
- May develop an adherent white or yellowish scale or hypertrophic horn (cutaneous horn).

## Typical Symptoms & Presentation
- Chronic mild soreness, localized tenderness, stinging, or irritation, particularly upon exposure to sunlight or gentle friction.
- Often arises in an area of diffuse UV damage (field cancerization) on sun-exposed skin: scalp of balding men, face, forehead, ears, dorsum of hands, and forearms.
- Highly prevalent in fair-skinned older adults (Fitzpatrick types I-II) with cumulative lifetime UV radiation or occupational outdoor exposure.

## Red Flags
- Substantial induration, localized thickening, rapid growth, or noticeable dermal elevation.
- Diameter exceeding 1 cm, localized pain or marked tenderness, or spontaneous ulceration/bleeding.
- These features raise high clinical suspicion for transformation into invasive keratinocyte carcinoma (SCC).

## When to See a Dermatologist
- Any rough, persistent rough scaly patch on sun-exposed skin that fails to resolve with standard moisturizers.
- Rapidly growing, painful, bleeding, or indurated scaly lesions requiring clinical assessment and potential field-directed (e.g., fluorouracil, imiquimod) or lesion-directed (cryotherapy, biopsy) intervention.
