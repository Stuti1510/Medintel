# Melanocytic Nevus (NEV)

## Definition
A melanocytic nevus (NEV, commonly referred to as a mole) is a benign, localized proliferation of melanocytes (the pigment-producing dendritic cells of the skin). Nevi can be congenital (present at birth) or acquired throughout childhood and adult life in response to genetic factors and sun exposure.

## Clinical Appearance
- **Junctional Nevus:** Flat, well-circumscribed macular brown-to-black pigmented spot with uniform pigmentation, located at the dermo-epidermal junction.
- **Compound Nevus:** Slightly raised, dome-shaped papule with regular pigmentation and smooth borders, extending from the junction into the dermis.
- **Intradermal Nevus:** Prominently elevated, dome-shaped, flesh-colored, light brown, or papillomatous soft nodule located entirely within the dermis, often seen on the face and neck.
- Symmetrical contour, sharply demarcated smooth borders, uniform shade of brown/tan, and stable size typically <6 mm.

## Typical Symptoms & Presentation
- Typically completely asymptomatic: no pain, bleeding, or spontaneous itching.
- Occasionally minor irritation from friction against clothing, jewelry, or shaving.
- Widely distributed across sun-exposed and sun-protected anatomical locations (trunk, extremities, head, neck).

## Red Flags
- The ABCDE criteria for melanoma screening:
  - **A (Asymmetry):** One half of the lesion does not match the other half.
  - **B (Border irregularity):** Edges are notched, scalloped, ragged, or poorly defined.
  - **C (Color variation):** Non-uniform pigmentation with variegated shades of tan, dark brown, black, red, white, or blue.
  - **D (Diameter):** Greater than 6 mm (though early malignant lesions can be smaller).
  - **E (Evolution):** The most critical red flag — any dynamic change in size, shape, surface elevation, color, or newly developed symptoms (spontaneous pruritus, bleeding, ulceration).
  - **"Ugly Duckling" sign:** A mole that looks distinctly different from all surrounding nevi on the same individual.

## When to See a Dermatologist
- Any mole displaying ABCDE characteristics, rapid growth, bleeding, or persistent itching.
- Individuals with a high nevus count (>50-100 nevi) or personal/family history of melanoma should undergo routine full-body dermatoscopic skin examinations.
